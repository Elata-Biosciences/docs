## What Elata is

Elata is a platform for building and using consumer-friendly biosensing, neuroscience, and cognitive applications that respect your privacy.

- **Elata** ([app.elata.bio](https://app.elata.bio)) is where people find and play these apps in the browser, and where builders publish them.
- **The [Elata SDK](/overview/elata-sdk)** is a set of open-source packages that turn a webcam or a consumer headband into a biosignal sensor, with processing that runs on the user's own device.
- **Privacy is built in.** Collected information stays on the users device. Anything shared beyond an app, such as the cross-app biometric Score, is opt-in and _can be turned off or deleted_ fully at the discretion of the user.

One thing that makes Elata vastly different than any other product on the market today is that you _don't need special hardware_ to begin using applications on the platform. 

Apps on Elata can turn an ordinary webcam or phone camera into a biosensor, using subtle changes in your face and behavior to measure or estimate signals like heart rate, heart rate variability, breathing rate, blink rate, affective state, and more.

We also support applications that can read brain activity over the web. These applications rely on consumer-grade EEG devices, such as [Muse](choosemuse.com).

<Note>
  Elata apps are for wellness, games, learning, and research. They are not
  medical devices and don't diagnose or treat any condition. See
  [Safety and limitations](/users/safety-and-limitations).
</Note>

## Building technology that’s open, transparent, and private

Elata is built around open-source software, transparent experimentation, and the idea that biosensing technology should be understandable, inspectable, extensible, and privacy-focused to the people using it.

Much of the technology behind Elata is developed publicly on GitHub, from the SDKs that power today’s apps to earlier experiments that helped shape what Elata has become.

A couple notable examples:

- [**Elata EEG**](https://github.com/Elata-Biosciences/elata-eeg) is our open-source effort to build a low-cost, right-to-repair EEG hardware device for neuroscience research and fun DIY Brain-Computer Interface (BCI) projects, such as the [brain-controlled gaming competition](https://x.com/DeSciWorld/status/1978762697022865576?s=20) we did in Singapore in 2025.
- [**ZORP**](https://github.com/Elata-Biosciences/zorp) is an experiment we did to initiate permissionless research infrastructure for collecting data, coordinating scientific studies, and rewarding participants.

Not every experiment becomes part of the current Elata product, but we keep this work public because useful ideas, code, and research shouldn’t disappear when a project changes direction.

You can explore our current and past work on [GitHub](https://github.com/Elata-Biosciences).

## How to read these docs

Every capability in these docs has a status, so you always know what you can use
today:

| Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Live** | Available now to everyone. |
| **Experimental** | Usable, but may change or have limits. For example, a package that is only available as source code. |
| **Planned** | Direction we're working toward. Not available, and details may change. |
| **Retired** | Existed before and has been removed. Kept here only so old links make sense. |

Pages that aren't fully live carry a tag in the sidebar. The full list is on
the [Status](/overview/status) page.
