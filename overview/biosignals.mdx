---

A **biosignal** is a measurable signal from the body, like a heartbeat or the
brain's electrical activity. Elata apps use three kinds, all from devices
people already own or can buy.

---

## Camera heart rate (rPPG)

**What it is.** Each heartbeat pushes blood into the skin, which changes its
color very slightly. A webcam can pick up that change, too small to see with
your eyes. This is called remote photoplethysmography, or rPPG.

**What it can estimate.** Pulse rate, and how steady the signal is.

**What affects it.** Movement, dim or flickering light, strong shadows, video
compression, and low frame rates all reduce accuracy. Accuracy can also vary
with skin tone and camera quality.

More detail: [Camera heart rate (rPPG)](/overview/science/rppg).

---

## EEG headbands

**What it is.** Electroencephalography (EEG) measures tiny electrical
signals from the brain through sensors on the scalp. Consumer headbands like
Muse use a few dry sensors on the forehead and behind the ears.

**What it can estimate.** How strong different rhythms are, such as alpha
waves, which tend to rise when you're relaxed with your eyes closed. Apps turn
these into measures like "calm" or "focus."

**What affects it.** Blinking, jaw clenching, movement, and poor sensor contact
create signals much larger than brain activity. A few sensors can't locate
activity precisely, and "calm" or "focus" scores are estimates, not direct
readouts of a mental state.

More detail: [EEG headbands](/overview/science/eeg).

---

## Headband PPG and heart-rate variability

**What it is.** Some headbands, like Muse S, have an optical sensor that
shines light into the skin and measures the reflection, the same principle as
a smartwatch heart-rate sensor (photoplethysmography, or PPG).

**What it can estimate.** Heart rate and **heart-rate variability (HRV)**: the
small variation in time between beats, which is often linked to recovery and
stress.

**What affects it.** Movement, a loose fit, and short recordings. HRV in
particular needs clean data over a minute or more to be meaningful.

More detail: [PPG and HRV](/overview/science/ppg-and-hrv).

---

## What these signals are not

<Warning>
  Elata apps are not medical devices. They don't diagnose, treat, or monitor
  any medical condition. If an app shows a reading that worries you, talk to a
  medical professional rather than relying on the app.
</Warning>

These measurements are useful for games, feedback, and personal exploration.
They are estimates that depend on conditions, and the same person can get
different readings on different days.

---

## Where processing happens

All three kinds of signal are processed on your device by the
[Elata SDK](/overview/elata-sdk). Raw camera frames and raw biosignals don't
need to leave your device. See [Privacy and consent](/overview/privacy-and-consent).
